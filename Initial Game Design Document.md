# Arthurian Expedition Game
## Initial Game Design Document

### High Concept

A 2D side-scrolling survival and resource-management adventure inspired by Arthurian legend.

The player begins as a weak and inexperienced young Arthur and gradually becomes the legendary King Arthur through the things he discovers rather than conventional character levels. Arthur acquires powerful equipment and relics, recruits legendary companions, learns hidden knowledge, and undertakes increasingly dangerous journeys into an Arthurian world that becomes more supernatural as the campaign progresses.

The game combines repeatable procedural **Expeditions** with authored **Campaign Quests**.

---

# Player Fantasy

The player begins with little power, knowledge, equipment, or reputation.

Over time, Arthur becomes legendary because of what he has accomplished and accumulated:

- Powerful weapons, armor, relics, and other equipment
- Legendary companions and Knights of the Round Table
- Knowledge taught by Merlin and discovered throughout the world
- Access to previously unreachable places and secret paths
- Completion of increasingly famous Arthurian adventures

Progression should be visible through the state of Arthur and his company rather than primarily through increasing numerical levels.

---

# Core Game Structure

The game has two primary types of adventure.

## Expeditions

Expeditions are repeatable, procedurally assembled survival journeys through the region associated with the player's current point in the campaign.

The primary goal is to travel outward from safety, survive increasingly dangerous encounters, discover useful items and knowledge, and successfully bring those discoveries home.

Distance traveled provides a simple measure of success and can support personal records and leaderboards.

The central tension is:

**How far can I safely travel and still make it home with what I find?**

## Campaign Quests

Campaign Quests are authored adventures that progress Arthur's story.

These contain more deliberate narrative content, unique encounters, puzzles, characters, locations, cutscenes, and major rewards.

Unlike Expeditions, Campaign Quests require the player to reach and complete a specific objective.

Campaign Quests gradually move Arthur through different regions and stages of Arthurian legend. Reaching a new stage of the campaign unlocks a new regional Expedition.

---

# Core Expedition Loop

1. Prepare Arthur and his company.
2. Choose equipment, supplies, relics, and companions.
3. Leave safety and begin traveling.
4. Resources are consumed as the company travels.
5. Procedural encounters interrupt the journey.
6. Make decisions using available resources, equipment, companions, and knowledge.
7. Discover loot, characters, information, locations, and secret paths.
8. Decide whether to continue farther or turn back.
9. Survive the return journey.
10. Successfully reach safety to permanently secure discoveries.
11. Use new discoveries to prepare for future Expeditions and Campaign Quests.

The farther the player travels, the greater the potential rewards and dangers.

---

# Presentation

The primary adventure view is a 2D side-scrolling journey.

The upper portion of the screen shows Arthur and his company traveling from left to right through illustrated regional backdrops.

The lower portion contains information and interaction such as:

- Party status
- Provisions and other resources
- Equipped items
- Current conditions
- Dialogue
- Encounter choices
- Combat actions
- Contextual information

Travel periodically stops when an encounter occurs.

The visual journey should increasingly communicate Arthur's progression. Early Arthur may travel alone and poorly equipped through relatively mundane countryside. Later expeditions may show mounted legendary knights carrying famous equipment through increasingly supernatural landscapes.

---

# Resources and Survival

Expeditions require the player to manage limited resources.

Potential resources include:

- Provisions
- Health and injuries
- Morale
- Gold
- Consumable items

Resources should create meaningful decisions rather than exist solely as meters that continuously decrease.

A larger and more powerful company may also be more expensive to sustain.

The player should frequently face situations where continuing farther is possible but dangerous.

---

# Encounters

Procedural encounters are the primary interactive events during Expeditions.

Examples include:

- Travelers
- Bandits
- Hostile knights
- Monsters
- Environmental hazards
- Ruins
- Shrines
- Villages
- Merchants
- Injured people
- Strange supernatural events
- Hidden locations
- Road forks
- Camps
- Puzzles

Encounter options depend on the current state of the expedition.

A problem might be solved differently because Arthur possesses a particular item, travels with a particular companion, has learned specific knowledge, or is willing to spend a limited resource.

There should not always be a universally correct decision.

---

# Risk and Resolution

Some uncertain actions may use visible dice rolls or similar probability systems.

Equipment, companions, conditions, and knowledge can modify the player's chances or provide entirely different solutions.

Different encounter types may use different forms of interaction. These can include:

- Choice-based decisions
- Dice checks
- Simple turn-based combat
- Environmental or logic puzzles
- Resource trades
- Knowledge checks
- Companion-specific actions

The game does not need one universal resolution mechanic if different interactions better serve different types of adventure.

---

# Combat

Combat is currently envisioned as a relatively simple turn-based encounter system rather than an action game.

Possible core actions include:

- Attack
- Defend
- Talk
- Use Item
- Companion Action
- Flee

Specific enemies may alter these rules or function partially as puzzles.

Combat should interact with the broader expedition system. Winning while suffering major injuries or consuming important resources may still represent a costly outcome.

The combat system requires further design.

---

# Push-Your-Luck and Returning Home

Loot discovered during an Expedition is **unsecured**.

The player must successfully return to safety before newly discovered equipment, relics, knowledge, and other rewards become permanent.

Equipment brought into the Expedition remains owned by the player even if the Expedition fails.

At any appropriate point, the player may choose to turn around.

Return travel still consumes resources and carries risk, but should be compressed compared with outward exploration so that returning does not require replaying the entire journey at normal speed.

This creates the central survival calculation:

**Going farther increases potential rewards, but every additional mile also makes returning safely more difficult.**

---

# Failure

If an Expedition fails, discoveries made during that Expedition are lost unless they were previously secured.

Arthur cannot permanently die.

Major legendary companions required for authored stories should also be protected from random permanent death. They may instead become wounded, captured, separated, cursed, or otherwise temporarily unavailable.

Minor followers and retainers may potentially die permanently.

The exact follower-death rules require further design.

---

# Progression

Arthur does not primarily progress through conventional XP levels.

Persistent progression is based on four major categories.

## Equipment and Relics

Weapons, armor, rings, charms, horses, tools, consumables, and legendary artifacts improve survival or create new possibilities.

The most interesting equipment should change what the player can do rather than merely provide larger numerical bonuses.

## Followers

Arthur gradually recruits companions, including legendary Arthurian knights.

Followers provide capabilities, encounter options, strengths, weaknesses, personalities, and potentially unique events.

## Knowledge

Arthur learns about the hidden world through Merlin, discoveries, manuscripts, encounters, and other teachers.

Knowledge can reveal solutions, interpret symbols, unlock routes, provide advantages during encounters, or allow Arthur to understand supernatural phenomena that were previously inaccessible.

## Accomplishments

Campaign progress and significant deeds can change how the world responds to Arthur and unlock new characters, quests, opportunities, and regions.

---

# Branching Paths and Secrets

Expeditions are not completely linear despite their side-scrolling presentation.

The company periodically encounters branching routes.

Different paths may offer different combinations of:

- Danger
- Distance
- Resources
- Encounter pools
- Rare discoveries
- Safe locations
- Secret areas

Some routes are initially inaccessible.

Items, relics, followers, knowledge, and previous accomplishments can reveal entirely new choices.

For example, an ancient ruin encountered repeatedly during early Expeditions might appear inaccessible until Arthur later obtains a relic bearing the same markings. Returning with that relic reveals a hidden route and an entirely new set of possible encounters.

A major progression principle is:

**Arthur's progression does not simply make his numbers larger; it makes more of the world accessible.**

---

# Regional Structure

Expeditions are procedurally generated according to the player's current campaign region.

Each region has its own:

- Visual environments
- Encounter pools
- Characters
- Enemies
- Resources
- Loot
- Secrets
- Branching locations
- Campaign-related discoveries

Campaign progression moves Arthur into new regions with greater danger and increasingly supernatural content.

The exact sequence of Arthurian regions and Campaign Quests still needs to be designed.

---

# Campaign Requirements

Major Campaign Quests may require Arthur to acquire specific discoveries before they can begin.

These requirements can encourage multiple Expeditions through the current region.

Required objects should not simply appear as guaranteed rewards at the end of an Expedition. Instead, they can enter the regional discovery pool and potentially be found through exploration, encounters, loot events, puzzles, or secret paths.

Finding an important campaign object creates an immediate survival decision because it remains unsecured until Arthur successfully returns with it.

---

# Camelot

Camelot serves primarily as an interactive home and narrative hub rather than a city-management game.

The player can return there between adventures to interact with characters, prepare equipment, choose companions, learn information, receive quests, and participate in story events.

Camelot may visually and narratively develop as Arthur's legend grows, but managing its construction or economy is not intended to be a major gameplay system.

---

# Arthurian and Educational Content

The game should introduce players to Arthurian legend through characters, places, stories, objects, and adventures rather than through detached educational material.

Historical, mythological, philosophical, and esoteric ideas can also appear through:

- Merlin's teachings
- Environmental symbolism
- Puzzles
- Manuscripts
- Characters
- Relics
- Mysteries
- Campaign stories

Knowledge should frequently have gameplay value.

The game can begin relatively grounded and gradually expose Arthur and the player to deeper supernatural and esoteric elements.

---

# Current Signature Mechanic

The current defining idea is the combination of **push-your-luck survival expeditions with discovery-based progression**.

Arthur travels progressively farther into procedurally assembled Arthurian regions while managing the resources necessary to survive both the outward and return journeys.

Items, followers, knowledge, and accomplishments gained along the way do more than increase power: they reveal new choices, solutions, secret paths, and areas of the world.

---

# Prototype Focus

The first prototype should validate the Expedition loop rather than attempt to build the entire campaign.

A useful initial playable slice would demonstrate:

- 2D side-scrolling travel
- A single regional environment
- Distance tracking
- Basic resource consumption
- Several procedural encounters
- Choice-based encounter resolution
- Loot discoveries
- At least one branching path
- At least one path unlocked by an item or knowledge requirement
- The decision to continue or return
- A compressed return journey
- Securing loot by successfully returning
- Expedition failure and restart
- A simple persistent inventory between Expeditions

Combat, dice systems, Camelot, extensive narrative content, and the full campaign can initially remain simplified unless needed to validate the central loop.

---

Major questions still to resolve include:

- Exact survival resources
- Encounter probability/resolution mechanics
- Combat design
- Dice versus other random-resolution systems
- Companion abilities and party limits
- Legendary versus minor follower rules
- Injury and morale systems
- Return-journey mechanics
- Loot rarity and generation
- How secret routes persist between Expeditions
- Safe locations outside Camelot
- Exact failure penalties
- Campaign-item discovery rules
- Leaderboards and scoring
- How much procedural content versus authored content each region contains
